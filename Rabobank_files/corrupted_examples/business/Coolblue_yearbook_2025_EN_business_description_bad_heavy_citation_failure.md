### Business model and operations

Coolblue’s business combines electronics retail and energy-related offerings through an omnichannel model that integrates digital channels, physical stores, customer service, and owned delivery and installation capabilities. Its strategy targets are NPS and EBITDA.

The company’s revenue base in FY2025 consisted of sale of goods of €2,431.3 million and marketing fees of €131.5 million, for total revenue of €2,562.8 million. Gross profit was €550.6 million, operating profit was €(3.3) million, and profit for the year was €1.2 million. Services revenue increased to €55.0 million from €44.8 million.

Operations are supported by an 88,000 m² Tilburg Package Park, described as a fully solar-powered warehouse, together with 22 hubs and 38 stores at end-2025. The Tilburg Package Park mechanization has reduced cardboard use by over 1,000,000 kilograms annually since 2021.

Coolblue also emphasizes service propositions such as delivery and installation, customer-service improvements, and sustainability-linked activities including fleet electrification, bike delivery, “Free Washing and Drying,” and refurbishment of washing machines and mobile phones.

At end-2025, the workforce totaled 5,628 employees, including 1,394 in delivery, 980 in stores, 877 in warehouse and returns, 1,509 in office roles, 396 in customer service, and 472 in technology roles. The company reported 128,684 job applications and 92 nationalities in 2025.

### Markets and strategy

Coolblue operates in the Netherlands, Belgium, and Germany. Management reported positive developments in the Netherlands and Belgium, growth in Germany, and a positive 2026 outlook.

A central strategic priority in 2025 was Germany expansion, alongside artificial intelligence (AI)-supported advisory content, delivery and installation, warehouse mechanization, energy-market developments, and customer-service improvements. In 2025, Germany revenue was €307 million.

The company reported that 2025 developments included 6 new German stores and 4 depots, but the named locations cited in the extract identify only Hamburg, Münster, and Hannover as stores and Langenhagen, Hamburg, Tamm, and Berlin as depots, creating a disclosed inconsistency in store count detail. Management stated that this expansion enabled service to over 40 million German customers and “half of Germany” with its own delivery proposition.

For 2026, Coolblue stated that it plans to accelerate Germany growth and open 10 stores. It also expects benefits from Tilburg Package Park mechanization, website improvements, expansion of Free Washing and Drying, and more energy-market opportunities.

Investment levels reflect these growth and capability priorities. In 2025, investment was €88.1 million. Net operating cash flow was €91.1 million, while net investing cash outflow was €51.2 million, including €63.2 million of property, plant, and equipment and €24.9 million of intangible assets.

### Principal disclosed risks and responses

Coolblue stated that risk management is embedded across the organization, with ultimate responsibility resting with the Executive Board, and that the framework covers strategic, operational, finance and reporting, and compliance risks. In 2025, it identified 11 risks, unchanged from 2024, merged “Competition” and “Economic conditions,” and added “Key commercial partnerships.”

The disclosed extract links risk exposure to the company’s strategy in areas including Germany expansion, exclusive brands, warehouse automation and mechanization, and energy supply. This suggests that growth execution, operational scaling, supply continuity, and participation in energy markets are important areas of management attention.

Reported responses include monitoring economic uncertainty, cyber and privacy controls with third-party assessments, continuity and fallback procedures, stock algorithms, supplier collaboration, safety procedures, and scenario planning for financing, liquidity, and energy-sourcing risks. In 2025, the company added key risk indicators (KRIs) and strengthened cybersecurity and fraud controls. For 2026, it plans further integration of risk types, new governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) tooling, and mechanization assessment.

Financial performance also indicates the cost of the company’s expansion phase. EBITDA declined to €86.5 million from €97.8 million in 2024 due to heavier investment, mostly in Germany. This connects the reported near-term earnings pressure to the group’s market expansion strategy and operating build-out in Germany.
