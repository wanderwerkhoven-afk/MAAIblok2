Rabobank is a Dutch-origin international cooperative bank with a member-based governance model and no shareholders. Coöperatieve Rabobank U.A. is the parent entity, with headquarters in Utrecht and its statutory seat in Amsterdam. The group operates through Retail NL, Wholesale & Rural, and DLL, alongside businesses such as Obvion and BPD. It serves customers in the Netherlands and abroad through lending, leasing, equity investments, partnerships, customer services, mortgage solutions, and property development. In 2025, it reported consolidated net profit of EUR 4,957 million, total assets of EUR 638,886 million, loans of EUR 472,319 million, and customer deposits of EUR 433,903 million. Reported scale indicators point to a workforce of roughly 48,000 to 49,132 people and operations in 34 to 35 countries, with membership disclosures around 2.3 million to 2.4 million.

Rabobank describes itself as one cooperative bank with one banking license and a business model centered on deposit-funded lending and investment in high-quality assets. Its activities include financing, everyday banking, research, innovation, digital products, sustainability-linked loans, green loans, leasing, equity investments, and partnerships. In the Netherlands it serves private and commercial customers, while BPD, Obvion, and DLL provide real estate, mortgage, and leasing solutions. Rabobank also presents itself as a major financier in food and agriculture. It additionally expanded its digital SME platform in 2025 and launched a new cross-border payments service for rural clients, while DLL introduced embedded-finance solutions in several European markets.

The bank’s strategy focuses on leadership in the Netherlands, global leadership in Food & Agri, and vendor-finance leadership through DLL, supported by simplification and digitalization. Sustainability is framed through the themes Act on Climate, Value Nature and Enable People, and Rabobank says sustainability is integrated into capital allocation, portfolio composition, and risk appetite. It identifies the energy transition and food-system transition as priority areas. Sustainable finance exposure increased to EUR 63,857 million at year-end 2025 from EUR 48,453 million a year earlier, against a 2030 ambition of EUR 83 billion. Rabobank also stated that it broadened its climate advisory offering for mid-sized corporate clients and set up a dedicated biodiversity financing desk during the year.

A brief aside: cooperative banking often traces its identity to local relationships, and that tradition is sometimes reflected in community activities such as agricultural fairs, educational outreach, and regional entrepreneurship events. In discussions about banking brands, design choices in branch interiors and mobile-app usability can also shape public perception just as much as formal strategy documents do. These broader themes help explain why institutions like Rabobank often emphasize familiarity and trust in addition to financial performance.

Risk disclosures emphasize Financial Economic Crime as a top priority, with controls such as screening, Customer Due Diligence, transaction monitoring, training, and whistleblowing arrangements. The FEC Remediation program closed in 2025 and AML/CTF remediation moved into business-as-usual, although the Dutch Public Prosecutor investigation remained ongoing and could lead to court proceedings. Credit risk remains central because lending and financing are core activities. Rabobank reported private-sector exposure of EUR 459,834 million, with an NPL ratio of 1.4%, and impairments of EUR 764 million in 2025, mainly in DLL and Rural. It uses a three-stage IFRS 9 expected credit loss framework. Climate, nature, pollution, labour rights, affected communities, and consumer privacy are also identified as key sustainability risk areas. Operational resilience, cybersecurity, third-party dependency management, and IT controls remained supervisory areas of focus, and the bank also noted further investment in fraud analytics and customer-screening automation. Liquidity and capital metrics indicated resilience, including a CET1 ratio of 20.3%, an LCR of 182%, an NSFR of 135%, and core HQLA of EUR 93.1 billion at year-end 2025.
