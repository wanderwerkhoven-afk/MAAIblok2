[Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 5] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 153] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 281]

- Rabobank serves customers in the Netherlands and internationally, with activities including lending, leasing, equity investments, partnerships, customer services, mortgage solutions, and property development. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 49-50] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 268]
- In 2025, Rabobank reported consolidated net profit of EUR 4,957 million, total assets of EUR 638,886 million, loans of EUR 472,319 million, and customer deposits of EUR 433,903 million. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 276-278] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 350]
- Reported scale indicators include over 48,000 employees in 34 countries, while other disclosures state offices in 35 countries and total headcount of 49,132 including non-employees; reported membership is also inconsistent at over 2.3 million, 2.4 million, and approximately 2.3 million. ### Business model and operations

Rabobank describes itself as a cooperative bank and international financial services provider operating on cooperative principles. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 21] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 380] It operates as one cooperative bank with one banking license. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 153]

Its business model is centered on deposit-funded lending and investment in high-quality assets. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 298] Activities include financing, everyday banking, research, networks, innovations, digital products, sustainability-linked loans, green loans, leasing, equity investments, and partnerships. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 49-50] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 78]

The group reports business lines as Retail NL, Wholesale & Rural, and DLL, while segment reporting presents five operating segments: Domestic Retail Banking, Wholesale & Rural, Leasing, Property Development, and Other Segments. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 15] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 328] This difference reflects a reporting distinction between business lines and financial segments. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 15] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 328]

In the Netherlands, Rabobank serves private and commercial customers, while BPD, Obvion, and DLL provide real estate, mortgage, and leasing solutions. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 268] Rabobank also describes itself as a large financier in the food and agriculture sector. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 132]

Property Development mainly comprises BPD in the Netherlands and Germany plus BPD Woningfonds; BEMOG was acquired in 2024 and Rabo SmartBuilds was transferred in 2025. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 42] In 2025, Property Development reported income of EUR 185 million and profit before tax of EUR 13 million, but net profit of negative EUR 51 million after a EUR 67 million impairment of a German deferred tax asset. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 42-43]

At 31 December 2025, total customer loans were EUR 472,319 million, gross customer loans were EUR 485,589 million, and private-sector loans were EUR 459,834 million. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 300, 303] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 329] Customer deposits were reported at EUR 433,903 million, with nearby disclosures of EUR 433,591 million, indicating a reported inconsistency. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 350] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 324] Rabobank stated that 66% of private-sector loans were in the Netherlands. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 303]

In 2025, total income was EUR 16,424 million, operating expenses were EUR 8,710 million, impairments were EUR 764 million, and net profit was EUR 4,957 million. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 26-28] Net interest income was EUR 12,001 million and net fee income was EUR 2,430 million. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 361]

### Markets and strategy

Rabobank’s strategy is to strengthen leadership in the Netherlands, global leadership in Food & Agri, and vendor-finance leadership through DLL, supported by simplification and digitalization. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 10, 16-17] Supervisory reporting also highlights sustainable growth, cost control, W&R Next, urban growth in Retail NL targeting younger customers, digitalization, and efficiency. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 166-167]

Sustainability is embedded in strategy through the themes “Act on Climate, Value Nature and Enable People,” and the bank states that sustainability is integrated into capital allocation, portfolio composition, and risk appetite. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 75] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 79] Rabobank identifies the energy transition and food-system transition as priorities for societal impact. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 18] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 24]

W&R financed EUR 8.2 billion of sustainable projects in 2025, including more than 60 solar and wind projects and 19 battery-storage projects. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 22] Rabobank also states that around 80% of project finance supports renewables in one disclosure and around 90% in another, which is a material inconsistency retained as reported. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 93-94] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 95]

Sustainable finance exposure increased to EUR 63,857 million at year-end 2025 from EUR 48,453 million at year-end 2024, against a 2030 ambition of EUR 83 billion. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 77] Rabobank also offered EUR 3 billion of transition financing to 2030, with outstanding balances rising from EUR 452 million in 2024 to EUR 1,206 million in 2025. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 117]

The bank uses 19 priority sector-by-region combinations covering 61% of financed emissions to steer toward 1.5°C alignment by 2050. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 268] Its 2030 financed-emissions indicator was about 29 MtCO2e in 2025 versus 32 MtCO2e in 2024. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 89] Rabobank states that the vast majority of its emissions are Scope 3 financed emissions. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 88]

Market-specific strategy disclosures show adaptation to sector transition pressures. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 199-200] In Dutch dairy, Rabobank highlighted regulatory pressure around nitrogen, water, and biodiversity, while 75% of corporate customers shared digital sustainability data and 3,683 customers were reviewed in 2025. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 199-200] In Dutch commercial real estate (CRE), it cited support from the energy-mix transition and stricter standards, but also constraints from policy gaps, district-heating and grid bottlenecks, higher rates, and stricter capital requirements; 66% of exposure had label A and 70% had verified label data in 2025. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 217-218]

### Principal disclosed risks and responses

Rabobank identifies Financial Economic Crime (FEC) as a top priority and material risk. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 147-149] Disclosed controls include screening, Customer Due Diligence (CDD), transaction monitoring, annual group-wide assessments, training, and whistleblowing arrangements. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 147-149] The FEC Remediation program closed in 2025 and Anti-Money Laundering/Counter-Terrorist Financing (AML/CTF) remediation moved into business-as-usual, but a Dutch Public Prosecutor criminal investigation remained ongoing with likely court proceedings. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 63] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 169] Benchmark litigation also remained contingent with no provision, and the impact of the AML/CTF indictment was described as unclear and potentially significant. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 327]

Credit risk remains central to reported operations because Rabobank’s core activities are lending and financing. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 49-50] Rabobank reported private-sector exposure of EUR 459,834 million, with Stage 1, Stage 2, and Stage 3 exposures of 85.9%, 12.5%, and 1.6%, and a non-performing loan (NPL) ratio of 1.4%. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 55-56] Impairment charges were EUR 764 million in 2025, mainly in DLL and Rural, while significant Expected Credit Loss (ECL) contributors included Brazil and Australia. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 55-56] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 309] Rabobank uses a three-stage International Financial Reporting Standard 9 (IFRS 9) ECL model and defines significant increase in credit risk using indicators such as rating deterioration, forbearance, 30 days past due, and vulnerable sector status. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 307] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 290]

Climate, nature, pollution, labour rights, affected communities, and consumer privacy are disclosed as key sustainability risk areas. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 84-85] Climate risks are embedded in risk management, supported by a dedicated climate team, customer-transition support, sector limits, risk-appetite changes, and monitoring of 1.5°C-aligned plans. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 93-95] Pollution risk was described as material, especially nitrogen exposure in Dutch dairy and animal protein, and this contributed to portfolio-specific management-adjustment provisioning. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 116]

Nature dependency was assessed using ENCORE-based analysis on 2022 figures covering 63% of total assets; within scope, about 85% of assets were rated highly to very highly dependent on ecosystem services. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 115] Nature was integrated into 12 sector-by-region plans in 2025. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 115] Water disclosures are inconsistent: one statement says water remained material only for negative impact, while another says water is material but no longer material from a risk perspective. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 83] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 118-119]

Operational and control risks are also emphasized. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 166] Supervisory focus in 2025 included resilience, stress testing, risk management, business continuity, cybersecurity, and third-party dependency management. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 166] PwC identified provisions and contingent liabilities related to non-compliance with laws and regulations, and Information Technology (IT) general controls, as key audit matters. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 413] PwC reported control deficiencies, especially in identity and access management, but concluded these had no material impact on the 2025 financial statements. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 413]

Liquidity and capital metrics indicate the bank’s disclosed financial resilience in supporting its operating model. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 298] Rabobank reported a Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio of 20.3%, a Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) of 182%, a Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) of 135%, and core High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) of EUR 93.1 billion at year-end 2025. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 27-28] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, p. 58] The CET1 ratio increased from 16.9%, mainly due to expected Capital Requirements Regulation 3 (CRR3) Risk-Weighted Asset (RWA) reduction and retained earnings. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 27-28]

Several reported taxonomy metrics are inconsistent. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 107-108] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 241-245] For example, 2025 Green Asset Ratio (GAR) stock was reported as EUR 34,066 million or 15.06%, while 2024 figures were shown both as EUR 23,958 million or 4.61% and as recalculated turnover and CapEx percentages of 10.91% and 11.04%; separate tables for 31 December 2024 also showed differing eligibility and alignment percentages. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 107-108] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 241-245] These inconsistencies affect comparability of disclosed sustainable-asset metrics. [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 107-108] [Annual Report 2025_Rabobank.pdf, pp. 241-245]
